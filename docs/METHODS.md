# Computational methods and reproducibility

The [repository README](../README.md) gives the current execution order. The manuscript supplementary material gives the STACI hydraulic and stationary-quality equations, stopping records, and independent EPANET comparison.

## Fixed-load design model

Anytown uses `networks/anytown/input/network_anchor_replay.inp` and `network_config_anchor_replay.m`. The two auxiliary basin sites R65/R165 are zero-demand terminal junctions during nominal optimization: fixed pipes 78/80 have zero flow. The terminal heads at nodes 65/165 must lie within 215 ± 0.5 ft (equal to the adjacent network-side heads at zero flow). R10 and pump 82 deliver all 0.282644079872 m³/s of requested demand. The pump and fixed connectors are not diameter variables. All 38 physical pipes have continuous 0.1–64 in diameter bounds. The 19 assessed junctions have 10–120 m pressure bounds; anchor heads have separate constraints. Internal 0.02-ft anchor and 0.02-m junction-pressure margins protect the EPANET replay near active bounds. The pressure conversion is 2.30787 ft/psi.

The frozen branch-hop weights use undirected physical topology and the shortest count of branching junctions from potential supply entries 20, 60, and 160. A demand node with a single outgoing pipe also counts as a branch. Weight is `1/(1+b)`. Including 60 and 160 represents their possible role as supplies during failure, even though they do not supply nominal flow. Flow direction does not enter the weight. Every objective uses the same nominal pressure bounds and 0.2 mg/L chlorine floor, at consumers whose requested demand exceeds `π(0.010 m)^2(0.01 m/s)/4 = 7.854×10⁻⁷ m³/s`.

Anytown's demand pattern is frozen at its initial 0.7 multiplier. Hazen–Williams head loss, 1 mg/L source chlorine, bulk decay −1 day⁻¹, and zero wall decay are imported from the INP. There is no tank-level evolution or diurnal loading. The final Tanyimboh–Templeman result folder is `results_staci_10_120_clsplit_v2/`, Anytown uses `results_staci_anchor_replay/`, and Balerma uses `results_staci_r38_anchor_r44_physical_0p1_64in_v1/`. Balerma's R38 junction is a zero-flow, 117 m pressure anchor during nominal optimization; R43, R44 and R88 remain physical supplies. All four physical reservoirs are restored for failure replay, and the six reservoir connectors are protected from closure. The R38-anchor result directory is the sole released Balerma design set.

## Search and storage

`run_uniform_nominal_optimizations.m` runs the six Anytown objectives with STACI in memory. Previously feasible vectors are rechecked under the common constraints before being used as warm starts. Every candidate changes the resident model's diameters, solves hydraulics, and computes stationary chlorine for the shared feasibility check; no candidate creates an INP or quality history. The 100-member GA has 150 stall generations, a 10,000-generation ceiling and a two-hour safeguard. Actual stop generation, evaluations, time, seed, and constraint violation are in each MAT record and `ga_stop_six.csv`. Finite GA stopping is not proof of global optimality.

`run_anytown_anchor_replay_pareto.m` saves a finite nondominated cost versus branch-hop archive. `run_anytown_anchor_replay_rankings.m` calculates five frozen pipe rankings and nested subset searches for all six objectives. Each `(objective, criterion, K)` result is cached separately; adding larger K values does not rerun smaller subsets. A feasible K=2 seed is retained for narrow two-pipe feasible regions. The prior feasible diameter vector and its cross-evaluated metrics are retained at larger K whenever an independent GA returns a worse optimized objective. The original-diameter K=0 state is a plotting reference even if infeasible under the nominal design limits.

The six full-network endpoints, ranked-subset studies, and Pareto search use the same nominal feasibility evaluator. The reported GA wall times describe their final search stages and exclude earlier warm-start discovery.

## Failure replay

`run_anytown_anchor_replay_outages.m` transfers each saved nominal diameter vector without rounding to `network_physical_reservoirs.inp`. R65/R165 are again physical 215-ft reservoirs and can inject or absorb water. For each of the 38 physical pipe closures, the availability states are both auxiliary reservoirs, R65 only, and R165 only. The unavailable reservoir is isolated by closing its fixed connector; the available reservoir retains the original 215-ft head. R10/pump stays active. This yields 114 equally weighted cases per design, with no assigned outage probabilities.

Each case uses pressure-driven hydraulics only; no DDA hydraulic solve is performed in the outage loop. The requested-demand denominator is read from the fixed-load input. PDA uses zero minimum pressure, required pressure 33 psi, and exponent 0.5. Water service is `sum(min(delivered_i,requested_i))/sum(requested_i)`. Chlorine-compliant service includes only delivered demand at nodes with at least 0.2 mg/L. STACI solves stationary chlorine on each failed hydraulic state. `summarize_anytown_anchor_replay_vulnerability.m` computes conditional tail severity and ranking capture. Worst 10% means the average of the 12 largest deficits among 114 Anytown cases. These are conditional service measures, not annual reliability probabilities.

Balerma bridge failures can disconnect junctions from every available reservoir. Its outage code writes a temporary source-connected INP, assigns zero service to the disconnected nodes, and uses a 200-iteration Newton budget. Six direct reservoir connectors are excluded from the 448 eligible pipe failures. The current study compares all four reservoirs available with R38 unavailable. Temporary files occur only during failure replay, never in optimization candidates.

## Independent verification

Independent EPANET checking is a required post-search release step for every displayed nominal vector, not a GA fitness call. `supplementary/validate_reported_nominal_designs.m` reconstructs fresh matched EPANET 2.2 and STACI models for all 1,369 current final, ranked-subset and selected Pareto rows, deduplicating identical vectors. It compares head, signed link flow and chlorine by ID, tests the EPANET-side pressure, anchor, diameter and consumer-chlorine constraints, and records numerical flags in `supplementary/results/nominal_design_validation/nominal_design_comparison.csv`. All 18 final designs pass EPANET constraints, but two Balerma designs exceed the 0.01 L/s flow-agreement flag; the released [audit guide](../supplementary/results/nominal_design_validation/README.md) gives each case and the ranked-subset exceptions. A zero-demand stagnant node can give a much larger all-node chlorine difference than the material-consumer difference, so the two are reported separately. The audit covers fixed-load nominal states; PDA pipe-failure replay is a separate calculation.

## Small-network branch-hop comparison

The Tanyimboh–Templeman Supplementary matrix uses the same six-objective panel order as Anytown. Its extra full-network branch-hop result optimizes all eight physical diameters. The frozen `1/(1+b)` weights start at physical supply entries 1 and 2; node 1 is directly modeled as the 50-m source reservoir and node 2 is a prescribed fixed inflow. `tanyimboh_branch_hop_weights.m` computes weights from the undirected pipe graph and demand exits without a hydraulic solve. `run_tanyimboh_branch_hop_optimization.m` applies the common TT diameter bounds, 10-m pressure floor, and MATLAB GA settings. Its saved MAT, weights CSV, GA stop CSV, and six-design EPANET replay make the additional panel auditable.
